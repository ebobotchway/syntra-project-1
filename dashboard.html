Who is involved

Blue = a named road-user group. Gray = not a specific group (Unknown, Other road user).

Key findings

Fixed text for all years 2017-2024; it does not change with the filters.

  • Passenger cars (70.2% of all accidents) and bicycles (24.8%) are involved most often. Pedestrians follow at 10.3%, vans 8.1%, mopeds 8.0%, motorcycles 6.8%, trucks 4.4%, buses 1.4%.
  • "Unknown" is 10.7%: for about one accident in ten the road user is not recorded.
  • Shares add up to more than 100% because one accident can involve two groups (136,144 accidents involve two different groups).

Read with care: these are raw counts. A group that is common on the road appears often for that reason alone; severity and traffic volume are not part of this graph.

1b

Annual change

Key findings

Fixed text for all years 2017-2024; it does not change with the filters.

  • Almost every group drops in 2020 (bicycles only -5.5%; buses -37.7% and pedestrians -30.4% most) and rebounds in 2021-2022.
  • By 2024 (2017 = 100): bicycles 115, mopeds 105, vans and buses 97, pedestrians 86, motorcycles 83, passenger cars 82, trucks 73.
  • Post vs pre (average per year 2022-2024 against 2017-2019): trucks -17.2%, motorcycles -12.9%, cars -12.2%, pedestrians -10.4%, buses -6.4%, vans +1.5%, mopeds +7.3%, bicycles +14.6%.
  • "Unknown" more than doubles (+113% from 2017 to 2024). Part of the decline of the named groups may be recording shifting into "Unknown"; the data cannot show that.

Read with care: "Covid-19" is my own label for the periods. These are accident counts, not rates, so fewer accidents could also mean less traffic.

1c

Months and seasons

Months (heatmap)

Colour = seasonal index (blue below an average month, red above; 100 = average). Numbers = the measure chosen above. The index is not corrected for month length: February has 28 or 29 days; use "Per day" to correct for that.

Seasons (grouped bars)

The number marks each group's highest season. Winter, spring, summer and autumn have 90-92 days, so an even share of 25% is approximate.

Key findings

Fixed text for all years 2017-2024; it does not change with the filters.

  • Motorcycles, bicycles and mopeds peak in the warm months and nearly vanish in winter: motorcycles have 13.0% of their accidents in winter and 32.6% in summer.
  • Pedestrians are the reverse: highest share in winter (28.0%), lowest in summer (21.3%), with a peak in December (index 123).
  • Passenger cars, vans and trucks are fairly even, with a mild autumn peak (about 27%).
  • Per day, January is the quietest month for all accidents (84.8 a day), not February (91.2); February's low index is partly its length.
  • In 2020-2021 the spring share is lower for every group (all accidents 20.6% against 25.1% before); from 2022 the pattern looks like before again. The data states no cause.

Read with care: shares and indexes describe WHEN a group's accidents happen, not how many there are, and not rates per amount of traffic. The seasonal hypotheses (riding in good weather, darker months for pedestrians) are not tested.

About and caveats

  • Data: Belgian road-accident open data 2017-2024 (289,532 accidents), translated to English. One row = one accident (the data has no ID to prove it).
  • Counting: an accident counts for a road-user group if that group is road user 1 or road user 2, once per accident. "Single road user" is not a road user.
  • Unknown (about 10% of accidents, growing over the years) and Other road user are separate lines and hidden by default.
  • Periods: pre (2017-2019), during (2020-2021) and post (2022-2024) "Covid-19" are my own definition; the data states no cause.
  • Counts, not rates: traffic volume is not in the data, so more accidents does not mean more risk per kilometre.
  • Seasons: meteorological, whole months only (the data has no day). December, January and February of the same calendar year are grouped.
  • Open questions: why empty coordinates jump in Brussels in 2024; the difference between accident classes "with fatalities" and "fatally injured".
  • Not done: urban vs rural. The data has only "built-up area", no official urban/rural classification (decision #55).
